Vamsi Krishna Pendyala

Ph.D. Candidate, Computer Science · Arizona State University

I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at Arizona State University, working in the Arizona Center for Integrative Modeling and Simulation (ACIMS) with Dr. Hessam Sarjoughian, in collaboration with Intel Corporation, Chandler, AZ. I work at the intersection of machine learning and modeling and simulation.

Discrete-event simulation models of semiconductor factories are accurate but slow at scale. My research builds a hybrid framework in which learned models approximate Parallel DEVS (PDEVS) models at increasing levels of abstraction:

  1. Output trajectories: Forecasting factory throughput and turnaround time with temporal models (TCN, TCNA).
  2. Trajectory generation: Generating complete trajectories from a factory configuration with conditional temporal diffusion.
  3. Component behavior: Learning the input/output events of individual factory components, so learned models can run inside the simulation.

Each model is judged with a multi-model, multi-metric evaluation, from point accuracy to event timing and semantics. Previously I interned at Qualcomm Research (benchmarking and quantized on-device generative models) and Intel (agentic AI tools for GPU simulation), and worked as a software engineer at Lemnisk and Epsilon.

Email · CV · Google Scholar · GitHub · LinkedIn

Research

How can learned models approximate PDEVS models of semiconductor factories, at which levels of abstraction, with what measurable fidelity, and how can they be combined with PDEVS models into a hybrid simulation that is both faster and trustworthy?
